Painting Company Far West Side, TX: How Sun, Rain, Color, and Old Paint Rules Shape a Good Paint Job

Paint is like a coat for your house. It keeps the sun and water off the walls. In San Antonio’s Far West Side, that coat has a tough job. This guide explains, in plain words, what the weather does to paint and what to check before you hire anyone.

Why Sun and Heat Matter for a Painting Company Far West Side, TX Homeowners Hire

South Texas gets hot, and the heat is not the same every year. Texas Public Radio reported that San Antonio had only 18 days at 100 degrees or more by early September 2026. That is a small number next to 2023, which was the worst year on record with 75 such days. The same report said daytime highs were still pushing 100 degrees in early September.

Why does this matter for paint? Strong sun shines on your walls day after day. Walls that face south and west get the most sun. Paint on those walls often looks tired first. So a good plan looks at each side of the house, not only the whole house at once.

Rain Can Hurt Paint Too

Heat is only half the story. The same Texas Public Radio report said 2026 was a fairly rainy summer. The city had a rain surplus of nearly five inches by early September, and more than 26 inches of rain had fallen at the airport that year. The report also said a possible strong El Niño could bring more wet days this fall and winter.

Water and paint do not get along when the water gets trapped. Paint sticks best to walls that are clean and dry. After a wet spell, a careful painter waits for the wall to dry before starting. Paint cans also list the weather range the paint needs, so ask your painter to follow it.

Light Colors Can Help Keep Your Home Cooler

Color is not only about looks. The U.S. Department of Energy’s Building America Solution Center explains that cool paint colors on outside walls can lower the heat that gets into a home. Think of a white shirt and a black shirt on a sunny day. The white one stays cooler.

Here is what that guide says in simple terms:

  • In warmer parts of the country, homes with cool roofs and walls had about 6% to 12% lower heating and cooling use in one large study.
  • East, south, and west walls usually gain the most from a more reflective color.
  • Taller homes with two or more floors tend to gain more, because more of the wall sits in the sun.
  • Choosing a light paint is often free, since it costs about the same as any other color.
  • The best time to switch is when the house needs paint anyway.

Dark colors can still work. The guide says special “cool color” coatings can reflect more heat while keeping a dark look. You can compare wall products in the Cool Roof Rating Council directory that the guide describes.

Older Homes and Lead-Safe Rules

Many homes in the Far West Side are newer, but not all of them are. If your home was built before 1978, there is one more thing to know. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) says about three-quarters of U.S. homes built before 1978 still have some lead-based paint. Peeling or chipped old paint can make lead dust, and that dust can harm children.

The EPA’s rule says anyone paid to disturb paint in a home built before 1978 must be certified in lead-safe work practices. This includes one-person businesses. The rule generally does not cover homeowners working on their own homes, but it does cover rentals and house flips. The EPA also offers a tool to find certified firms.

Quick tip: If your home is from before 1978, ask for proof of lead-safe certification before you sign anything.

What Good Prep Work Looks Like

The paint is only the last step. Most of the work happens before the first coat. A careful crew will usually do these things:

  1. Wash dirt, dust, and mildew off the walls.
  2. Scrape off loose, peeling paint and sand rough spots.
  3. Fix cracks and holes, and replace worn caulk around windows and trim.
  4. Prime bare spots so the new paint can grip.
  5. Let everything dry, then apply the finish coats as the label says.

How to Choose a Painting Company Far West Side, TX Homeowners Can Trust

Now you know what the weather does and what good prep looks like. Next, learn how to choose a painting company Far West Side, TX, and ask each one the same questions. This makes it easy to compare them fairly.

  • What prep will you do? Ask for the steps in writing.
  • Which paint will you use, and why? A good answer mentions sun, heat, and moisture.
  • Can you suggest cooler colors? Ask about light colors or reflective “cool color” options.
  • Is my home older than 1978? If yes, ask for lead-safe certification.
  • What happens if it rains? A clear plan for wet days is a good sign.

Conclusion

A good paint job in the Far West Side comes down to a few simple ideas. Plan for strong sun on the south and west walls. Watch out for wet weather. Think about lighter or “cool” colors to help keep the house cooler. If your home is older, make sure the crew follows lead-safe rules. And always look for careful prep, because that is where most of the work happens. Ask clear questions, get the plan in writing, and you will be in good shape.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Texas sun really change how paint lasts?

Yes, strong sun is hard on exterior paint, and the walls facing south and west get the most of it. That is why a painter should check each side of your home. Prep and a good paint choice help a lot.

Are light paint colors better for hot weather?

Often, yes. The Building America Solution Center says lighter, more reflective colors keep walls cooler, and many light paints cost no more than other colors. Special cool-color coatings can also help darker colors reflect more heat.

When do I need a lead-safe certified painter?

If your home was built before 1978 and you are paying someone to disturb the paint, the EPA says that person or firm must be certified. Homeowners doing their own work in their own homes are generally not covered, but the EPA still suggests safe work habits.

Should I paint right after a rainy week?

It is usually better to wait. Paint sticks best to clean, dry walls, and damp walls can cause trouble later. Follow the weather range on the paint can and let your painter set the best day.

What should be in a painting quote?

Ask for the prep steps, the paint name, the number of coats, the areas being painted, and the plan for wet weather. If your home is older than 1978, also ask about lead-safe certification.
